Why Some Revisionist States Build and Others Break: Domestic Constraints, Time Horizons, and Strategic Choice in China and Russia

Ji Hwan Lee

M.A. Thesis
California State University, Fullerton
2026

PDF DOI

Abstract

This thesis examines why the strategic form of revisionist behavior varies across episodes. Existing scholarship shows that revisionism is not a single or self-evident category, but it pays less attention to why some revisionist challenges take institutional forms while others take coercive ones.

I argue that domestic conditions, especially economic performance and leader political security, shape leaders' time horizons and risk calculations, which in turn influence whether revisionist behavior takes the form of institution-building or coercion.

To evaluate this argument, the thesis employs a comparative case study with process tracing, focusing on China's creation of the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) and Russia's annexation of Crimea.

The analysis suggests that relatively permissive domestic conditions in China were more compatible with a longer-horizon strategy of institution-building, whereas weaker economic conditions and greater political insecurity in Russia were more compatible with a shorter-horizon logic in which coercion became politically more attractive.

The thesis does not claim that domestic conditions replace systemic, geopolitical, or ideational explanations. Rather, it shows that domestic conditions help explain why different strategic forms of revisionist behavior became politically feasible and attractive across these two episodes.
